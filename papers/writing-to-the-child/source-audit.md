# Source audit and verification queue

Working file for `draft-v1.md`. Ordered by what blocks submission first.

Two marker types are used in the draft and both are greppable:

```
grep -n "\[VERIFY:" draft-v1.md
grep -n "\[SOURCE NEEDED:" draft-v1.md
```

`[VERIFY: ...]` means a source that is believed to exist and whose bibliographic detail is unconfirmed. The argument attached to it survives if the detail is corrected.
`[SOURCE NEEDED: ...]` means there is no candidate source. The argument attached to it does not survive without one.

## Blocking. The draft cannot be submitted with these open.

**1. The race and description claim.**
Section: *What direct address does to content*. The claim that evaluative descriptors appear disproportionately in records about Black and mixed heritage families. This is the highest-risk sentence in the paper. It is stated in the draft as a concern the paper cannot source, which is defensible, but a reviewer will ask why it appears at all if it cannot be evidenced. Either find the study or cut the distributional version and keep only the mechanism, which stands on its own.

**2. Mette Larsen and the Danish principles.**
Sections: *Introduction*, *Why jurisdictions differ*. Currently sourced to an unpublished seminar deck. A comparative argument with one of its three legs resting on an unpublished deck will not pass review. Either locate a publication, or recast Denmark as a practice example explicitly attributed to an unpublished source, or drop the comparative section to a research proposal.

**3. MIRRA outputs.**
Sections: *Introduction*, *Access, redaction and the second project*. The paper leans on MIRRA more than on any other source, and currently has no citable output. Needed: which outputs exist, authorship order, titles, years, and which specific findings belong to which output. The two findings the draft uses are (a) files as fragmentary and deficit-dominated, (b) the access process itself as distressing. Both need attaching to a named output.

**4. White, Hall and Peckover.**
Section: *What direct address does to content*. This carries the systems argument, which is the theoretical link between mode of address and what practitioners can think. Your audit flagged it as worth the effort and I agree: it is the only thing in the paper that explains why register reform reaches the smallest part of the document.
